## Related Work

The rapid advancement of transformer architectures has profoundly impacted audio processing, yielding breakthroughs across self-supervised representation learning, multimodal fusion, music generation, and environmental sound understanding. This section reviews recent contributions organized by two complementary subtopics: self-supervised and multimodal pretraining of audio transformers, and transformer-based models for music and environmental sound understanding.

**Self-supervised and multimodal pretraining of audio transformers.** The self-supervised pretraining paradigm has become a cornerstone of modern audio representation learning, enabling models to leverage large quantities of unlabeled data prior to fine-tuning on downstream tasks. Atito et al. [1] proposed ASiT, a self-supervised framework for audio spectrogram representation learning that captures both local and global contextual information through group masked model learning and self-distillation. Evaluated on audio event classification, keyword spotting, and speaker identification, ASiT achieved state-of-the-art results on five audio and speech classification benchmarks, outperforming methods that rely on additional datasets for pretraining. Building on the Audio Spectrogram Transformer (SSAST) architecture, a dual-representation variant [2] introduced multi-layer feature fusion and pooling combinations, further boosting audio classification accuracy across multiple benchmarks. While ASiT and the SSAST variant operate within the unimodal audio domain, HiCMAE [3] extended the masked autoencoding framework to the multimodal setting. This hierarchical contrastive masked autoencoder employs skip connections and cross-modal contrastive learning for audio-visual emotion recognition, demonstrating superior performance on nine emotion recognition datasets. Together, these works illustrate a progression from unimodal to multimodal self-supervised learning, with ASiT emphasizing local–global representation trade-offs, the SSAST variant optimizing feature aggregation strategies, and HiCMAE exploiting cross-modal synergies.

**Transformer-based models for music and environmental sound understanding.** Beyond speech and classification tasks, transformer architectures have been increasingly applied to music generation and general audio synthesis. The ASiT framework [4], while originally designed for event classification, also provides a strong self-supervised backbone for environmental sound understanding, leveraging the same group masked modeling and self-distillation principles to learn transferable audio representations. In the domain of music generation, He et al. [5] introduced MelodyTransformer, a modified Transformer architecture for lyric-to-melody generation that incorporates a dual omnidirectional self-attention module, learnable positional encoding, and a melody-aware loss function. These design choices allow the model to better capture structural relationships and temporal order, improving both the coherence and the innovation of generated melodies over prior Transformer-based approaches. Addressing the broader challenge of general audio generation, Haji-Ali et al. [6] tackled scalability in ambient sound generation by introducing AutoReCap-XL, the largest ambient audio-text dataset with over 47 million clips, and GenAu, a scalable transformer-based latent diffusion architecture scaling up to 1.25 billion parameters. GenAu achieved significant improvements in Fréchet Audio Distance, Inception Score, and CLAP score over prior audio generators, highlighting the importance of jointly scaling data and model capacity. Whereas MelodyTransformer focuses on conditioned symbolic music generation with architectural modifications to self-attention, GenAu targets open-domain ambient audio synthesis through latent diffusion and large-scale pretraining.

**Open challenges and research gaps.** Despite these advances, several challenges remain. The integration of self-supervised pretraining with efficient transformer variants suitable for real-time or resource-constrained deployment is still underexplored. Cross-modal pretraining, while promising for emotion recognition, has yet to be systematically evaluated across a broader range of audio-visual tasks such as sound event localization or audio-visual speech enhancement. Furthermore, the scaling laws governing audio generation models warrant further investigation, as the substantial computational demands of large transformer-based generators may limit accessibility and reproducibility. Addressing these gaps—bridging self-supervised learning with efficient architectures, expanding multimodal evaluation, and characterizing scaling behavior—will be crucial for advancing toward more general, robust, and deployable audio intelligence systems.

## Proposed Novelties

| # | Novelty | Description | Difficulty | Rationale |
|---|---------|-------------|------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Lightweight self-supervised audio transformer via knowledge distillation** | Distil the ASiT [1] or SSAST-variant [2] teacher into a compact student transformer (e.g., using attention-transfer or contrastive distillation), retaining the local–global representational power while reducing parameters and latency for on-device audio classification. | ★★ Medium | Directly addresses the first open challenge: integrating self-supervised pretraining with efficient architectures. [1] and [2] provide strong unimodal backbones but were not designed for resource-constrained deployment; distilling them yields a practical model for real-time keyword spotting and event detection on edge hardware. |
| 2 | **Cross-modal audio-visual pretraining for sound event localization** | Extend HiCMAE’s [3] hierarchical contrastive-masked autoencoder to incorporate spatial cues (e.g., binaural or microphone-array signals) and evaluate on sound event localization and detection (SELD) benchmarks, beyond the emotion-recognition tasks originally reported. | ★★★ Hard | Fills the gap of cross-modal pretraining being underexplored outside emotion recognition [3]. SELD requires joint audio-spatial understanding and would benefit from the skip-connection and contrastive mechanisms that HiCMAE pioneered, but demands new positional encoding and multi-channel input handling. |
| 3 | **Scaling-law study of latent diffusion transformers for audio generation** | Conduct a controlled scaling analysis (model size, data size, compute) of GenAu-style architectures [6], measuring FAD, IS, and CLAP score across 3–4 model scales (e.g., 100M–1.25B params) to derive empirical scaling coefficients, analogous to those known in NLP and image generation. | ★★ Medium | GenAu [6] demonstrates the benefits of joint data–model scaling but does not characterize the scaling exponents. Such a study would provide reproducible guidelines for practitioners (third open challenge) on how to allocate resources when building large audio generators. |
| 4 | **Unified self-supervised backbone for music and environmental audio** | Pretrain a single transformer on a mixture of music and environmental sound corpora using the ASiT group-masked objective [1][4], then fine-tune on music genre classification, instrument recognition, and environmental sound event detection to test transfer across domains. | ★★ Medium | ASiT [1][4] was evaluated only on speech and environmental sound benchmarks; MelodyTransformer [5] targets symbolic music. A unified backbone would bridge the two surveyed subtopics and reveal whether a common self-supervised strategy suffices for both musical and non-musical audio. |
| 5 | **Real-time ambient sound synthesis via distilled GenAu** | Apply progressive knowledge distillation to shrink the 1.25B-parameter GenAu [6] into a sub-100M student that runs in real time on a single GPU, while preserving perceptual quality (FAD, IS) as measured against the teacher. | ★★★ Hard | Combines the first and third gaps: it makes large-scale audio generation deployable while testing whether scaling-driven improvements survive compression. No work to date has compressed a transformer-based latent diffusion audio generator, making this a high-impact yet challenging direction. |
