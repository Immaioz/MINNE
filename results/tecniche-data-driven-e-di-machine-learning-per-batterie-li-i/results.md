## Related Work

Recent advances in lithium-ion battery diagnostics have been propelled by the convergence of deep learning architectures, physics-informed modeling, and transfer learning techniques, each addressing distinct aspects of battery management—from state-of-charge (SoC) and state-of-health (SoH) estimation to remaining useful life (RUL) prediction and thermal runaway (TR) early warning. This section reviews the state of the art organized by these thematic areas, highlighting how different methodological paradigms relate to and complement one another.

**State of charge estimation using deep learning and recurrent neural networks.**  

SoC estimation has been a central focus, with recurrent neural network (RNN) variants forming the backbone of many recent approaches. Guo et al. [2] proposed a robust data-model combined driven method that integrates a gated recurrent unit (GRU) network with a square-root cubature Kalman filter under the generalized maximum correntropy criterion, demonstrating resilience against non-Gaussian measurement noise across diverse operating conditions. Targeting on-board deployment, Zhao et al. [3] developed a lightweight hybrid model combining a residual network (ResNet) with a gated recurrent neural network; the residual structure mitigates gradient degradation while the GRU captures temporal dependencies, achieving high accuracy with reduced computational complexity suited for resource-constrained battery management systems. Zhang et al. [4] introduced a dual-RNN framework that updates model parameters on a long time scale via one RNN and estimates SoC on a short time scale via an adaptive RNN, leveraging the Adagrad algorithm and fuzzy control; their approach reduced computation time by 66% while improving accuracy by 30% compared to gated RNN methods. Sridharan et al. [5] combined Residual WaveNet with LSTM and GRU networks to handle varying temperature conditions, showing that dilated convolutions and residual connections capture complex temporal patterns while LSTM and GRU respectively manage long-term dependencies and sequential processing efficiency. Chen et al. [6] proposed an end-to-end scalable deep recurrent structure integrating convolutional and recurrent neural networks with a Gaussian process regression layer for uncertainty-quantified SoC estimation, incorporating a non-parametric fast transfer learning strategy that adapts across temperature conditions two orders of magnitude faster than conventional fine-tuning. Collectively, these studies illustrate a progression from hybrid filter–neural approaches toward architectures that jointly optimize accuracy, computational efficiency, and cross-condition generalizability.

**State of health and remaining useful life prediction via data-driven models.**  

Parallel advances in degradation prognostics have produced a diverse toolkit for SoH estimation and RUL prediction. Wang et al. [7] proposed a physics-informed neural network (PINN) that models degradation dynamics from empirical degradation and state-space equations, validated on 387 batteries from multiple manufacturers with a mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 0.87%. Chen et al. [8] developed a deep learning framework for joint SoH and RUL prediction using only a single cycle of charging data; their CNN-PCC feature extractor fuses two-dimensional CNN features with handcrafted features into multi-scale hybrid representations, reducing RMSE by 35.3% and 37.3% for SoH and RUL, respectively, over single-task models. Oyewole et al. [9] introduced DG-PNUTS, an uncertainty-aware framework integrating dual GRU networks with a physics-informed Bayesian No-U-Turn Sampler (NUTS), leveraging Bayesian multi-source domain adaptation for generalizability across different battery chemistries and providing reliable uncertainty quantification. Chae et al. [10] proposed an allied denoising CNN (DnCNN) and CNN framework that removes ambient noise via residual learning, combined with feature fusion and Bayesian optimization to enhance robustness under real-world noisy operating conditions. Zhao et al. [11] developed an AT-CNN-BiLSTM architecture incorporating an attention mechanism to weight highly relevant multidimensional parameters (voltage, current, temperature), achieving R² values exceeding 0.991 and MAPE below 0.9%. These works demonstrate a clear trend toward multi-task and uncertainty-aware frameworks that move beyond point predictions to provide probabilistic degradation estimates.

**Transfer learning and domain adaptation for battery diagnostics across chemistries and operating conditions.**  

A critical bottleneck for deploying diagnostic models across diverse battery types is the domain shift introduced by different chemistries, form factors, and operating conditions. Badfar et al. [12] introduced a regression-based unsupervised domain adaptation method for cross-chemistry SoC estimation that requires no labeled target data, outperforming conventional classification-based domain adaptation and revealing trends in transfer capability across battery types. Yang et al. [13] proposed a cross-chemistry SoH prediction framework enabling knowledge transfer from lithium-ion to sodium-ion batteries using relaxation voltage features; multi-layer maximum mean discrepancy alignment with fine-tuning achieved average RMSE values as low as 0.65% using only 5% labeled target data across five distinct chemical systems. Hong et al. [14] developed a transfer learning-enhanced physics-informed neural network (TL-PINN) that combines physical constraints with domain adaptation for SoC estimation across NMC, LFP, and NCA chemistries, reducing RMSE by over 63% compared to the extended Kalman filter and over 50% compared to RNN models under data-scarce conditions, while maintaining prediction error within 2% under noisy measurements. Lin et al. [15] proposed a transfer learning SoH estimation method (TL-ST-LSTM) using explainable electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) data and a spatiotemporal LSTM network, achieving RMSE within 1.9% and improving accuracy by over 80% compared to non-transfer baselines across different temperatures and cell types. Wu et al. [16] combined offline EIS with a domain-adversarial neural network (DaNN) for transfer learning across battery types and aging conditions, incorporating a feature search strategy and Gaussian process regression to achieve RMSE below 1% with significantly reduced dependency on extensive measurement data. A consistent finding across these studies is that hybrid strategies—pairing domain alignment with either physical constraints or impedance-based features—yield superior data efficiency and generalization compared to purely data-driven transfer.

**Physics-informed neural networks for hybrid battery modeling.**  

PINNs have emerged as a powerful paradigm that embeds physical laws into data-driven architectures to improve generalization and interpretability. Wang et al. [17] demonstrated the effectiveness of PINNs for battery degradation modeling, achieving a MAPE of 0.87% on a large dataset of 387 batteries and showing strong performance in transfer and small-sample experiments. Hassanaly et al. [18] introduced a PINN surrogate to replace physics-based battery models (single-particle and pseudo-2D models) for rapid parameter inference; a multi-fidelity hierarchical training strategy, in which multiple neural networks are trained with varying physics-loss fidelities, significantly improved surrogate accuracy by training solely on governing-equation residuals, making high-throughput Bayesian calibration tractable. Tian et al. [19] presented a generic PINN framework with a two-branch structure for SoH estimation, where one branch processes extracted health indicators while the other simulates solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) growth using universal differential equations, achieving a MAPE of 0.97% across four datasets with excellent generalization in small-sample and transfer-learning scenarios. Wang et al. [20] proposed a PINN framework for identifying microscopic parameters of lithium-ion battery electrochemical models, employing three neural networks operating in parallel with a parameter categorization approach that reduces the search-space dimension. Yang et al. [21] developed a multi-task health management framework combining multi-task processing with a PINN for joint SoH estimation, RUL prediction, and short-term degradation path forecasting, achieving a SoH estimation MAPE of 0.75%, a short-term degradation path normalized deviation of approximately 0.01, and an RUL prediction MAE of 104 cycles. These works highlight a growing emphasis on embedding mechanistic knowledge—whether from SEI growth models, single-particle physics, or electrochemical theory—into neural architectures to enhance data efficiency and physical consistency.

**Data-driven anomaly detection and thermal runaway prediction.**  

Ensuring battery safety through early detection of anomalous behavior and TR prediction has become an increasingly active research frontier. Masalkovaitė et al. [22] employed a transfer learning support vector machine approach to estimate heat release variability during thermal runaway using only ejected mass measurements and cell metadata, demonstrating that low-cost mass measurements can replace expensive calorimetry for safety characterization of commercial Li-ion cells. Das Goswami et al. [23] developed a combined multiphysics and deep learning framework in which convolutional neural networks classify thermal runaway into three stages (safe, critical, and actual TR) from simulated thermal images, while a YOLO object-detection model localizes high-temperature hotspots. Daniels et al. [24] used a random forest classification model to predict the faulty cell position in air-cooled cylindrical Li-ion battery modules under thermal runaway conditions, optimizing temperature sensor layouts via a heat-map approach for aligned, staggered, and cross cell arrangements. Huang et al. [25] proposed a ConvTransformer model integrating CNN, Transformer, and MLP modules to predict the remaining time before thermal runaway under high charge–discharge rates, providing accurate real-time early warnings. Liu and Li [26] introduced a whole-feature neural network (WFNN) for real-time TR monitoring using measured temperature, current, and voltage signals, achieving 99.48% monitoring accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for real-time deployment. These studies collectively illustrate a shift from post-mortem analysis toward predictive and real-time safety monitoring, leveraging both physics-based simulation and data-driven classification.

**Open challenges and research gaps.**  

Despite these substantial advances, several challenges remain. First, while transfer learning techniques have demonstrated impressive cross-chemistry generalization, most studies still rely on fine-tuning or domain adaptation strategies that require at least a small amount of labeled target data; fully unsupervised cross-domain battery diagnostics without any target labels remains an underexplored area [12], [13]. Second, although physics-informed neural networks offer improved interpretability and data efficiency, the integration of detailed electrochemical models into PINN frameworks is still computationally intensive and has primarily been demonstrated for parameter inference rather than real-time estimation [18], [20]. Third, the joint estimation of SoC, SoH, and RUL within a unified framework remains challenging due to the different time scales of these phenomena; while recent multi-task and dual-timescale approaches have made progress [4], [21], a truly integrated solution that simultaneously handles all three quantities with rigorous uncertainty quantification across diverse operating conditions has yet to be established. Fourth, thermal runaway prediction methods have largely been validated on simulated or controlled laboratory data, and their robustness under real-world field conditions—including sensor noise, communication delays, and varying thermal management strategies—requires further investigation [23], [25]. Finally, the computational constraints of embedded battery management systems continue to motivate the development of lightweight yet accurate models; achieving a favourable trade-off between model complexity and prediction accuracy across all diagnostic tasks remains an active area of research [3], [6], [26]. Addressing these gaps will be critical for the next generation of intelligent, safe, and universally deployable battery management systems.
